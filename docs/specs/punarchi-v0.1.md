# Punarchi v0.1 source and scope contract

Status: source audit in progress. This document does not specify an executable
rule set, and the planned golden suite remains inactive. The first profile is
Nannūl Eḻuttatikāram-root-text-scoped; its merger rules are blocked on a
full rule inventory, printed-page/variant review, and expert-reviewed test
vectors. The chapter boundaries and primary edition are now pinned.
Lexicon provenance and redistribution rights are a separate prerequisite for a
future lexical splitter.

## Decision and first milestone

Use the Nannūl Eḻuttatikāram root text as the primary authority for the first
named literary Punarchi profile. This follows the toolkit's stated Nannūl
merger scope and provides a bounded, explicit grammar tradition. The Project
Madurai text divides its Eḻuttatikāram into Uyirīṟṟup Puṇariyal (151–203),
Meyyīṟṟup Puṇariyal (204–239), and Urupu Puṇariyal (240–257). This organization
helps inventory and scope implementation; it does not make Nannūl a universal
or contemporary usage authority.

Nannūl is selected as the module's actual rule source because this feature is
specified as a Nannūl merger, and Nannūl is a distinct grammatical treatment:
comparative scholarship records that it retains, simplifies or omits some
Tolkāppiyam rules and adds others. Implementing Tolkāppiyam and calling it
Nannūl would therefore be inaccurate. Tolkāppiyam remains necessary to explain
rule lineage and differences, but its rules belong to a separate profile.

The primary edition is A. Thamodaran's 1999 critical edition of the Nannūl
root text with Sankara Namasivayar's Viruttiyurai, corrected by Sivagnana
Munivar, published by the World Tamil Research Institute. TVA's title page
identifies this imprint; TVA's bibliographic page gives the English publisher
name as International Institute of Tamil Studies, publication 330, first
edition, 1999, 600 pages. Its editorial preface (viewer page 72) says the
editor compared manuscript and printed witnesses and distinguishes the root,
commentary, and Munivar's corrections. TVA viewer page 263 presents Nannūl
sūtras 204–205 with their commentary and examples as separate layers in
formatted HTML text. The inspected HTML does not include scan images. This is
the strongest inspected, traceable basis for the first profile, while
print-level readings and pagination still require a scan or physical copy of
the 1999 edition before promoting any individual rule to a fixture.

Project Madurai's Nannūl e-text is retained as an independent numbering and
contents cross-check. Its header says it follows the edition edited by Mani
Thirunavukkarasu Mudaliar and published by Vavilla Ramasamy Sasthulu & Sons,
Madras, 1926. It is not the primary citation edition. If its file is ever
redistributed, preserve its required header. Kāṇṭikaiyurai by Āṟumuka Nāvalar
is a separate commentary witness, not interchangeable with Viruttiyurai.
Never attribute commentary wording or examples to the root sūtra.

Tolkāppiyam remains the foundational comparative source, not a discarded
source. The source review found no single Tolkāppiyam edition ready to pin as
sole authority. The TVA scan of the 1979 Annamalai Eḻuttatikāram with
Iḷampūraṇar's commentary, edited by Ku. Sundaramurthy, has been inspected; its
title verso says second edition, August 1979. Its front matter discusses
earlier editions and corrections, but the inspected pages do not establish a
systematic manuscript-collation method. Keep it as a provisional
page-citation witness only.

The 1996 *Textual Variation of Tolkappiyam: A Deep Study* (CICT's English
catalog title), edited by K. M. Vēṅkaṭarāmaiyā, S. V. Subramanian, and P. V.
Nākarājan, is the strongest
verified textual-variation resource found. Its editors discuss manuscript and
printed witnesses and provide a substantial apparatus, but explicitly state
that they are not establishing one base reading. Use it to expose and track
variants, not as the definitive root-text edition.

The same-editor Annamalai edition has later records: Thiagarajar College
cites a 1992 third edition, and CICT's Semmozhi catalogue lists a matching
title/editor/publisher as 1996 plus duplicate 1991 records. No title-page or
preface scan was located for the later copy, so revision versus reprint versus
cataloging discrepancy remains unresolved. Obtain and inspect that copy before
pinning one commentary edition. The TVA 2003 Tamilmann scan separates root
sūtras, Iḷampūraṇar commentary, and added explanation in its sampled pages,
but the sample did not establish a critical apparatus. The 1982 WTRI
*Puṇariyal Uraivaḷam* is an A. Sivalinganar-edited compilation of commentaries,
useful for interpretation rather than as a root-text edition. Later sources
include Balasundaram's 2012 research commentary, Subramanian's revised 2021
commentary, and CICT's 2025 English research monograph released in 2026; none
has been established here as the Tamil root-text authority.

Current working arrangement: cite the 1979 scan only provisionally and
cross-check any eventual Tolkāppiyam passage against the 1996 variant study.
No rule number, reading, interpretation, or Nannūl equivalence is approved for
use until a specific comparison passage and its commentary layer are reviewed.
If a rule requires comparison with Collatikāram, select and inspect a separate
edition for that work before citing it. The section map below is a discovery
index, not a one-to-one equivalence: compare individual rules and record where
Nannūl retains, changes, omits, or adds a rule. Never blend the traditions
under an unqualified `punarchi` mode.

Project Madurai's Nannūl contents list these chapter-local ranges; the TVA
critical edition's viewer page 263 independently shows root sūtras 204–205 in
the Meyyīṟṟup section. These facts verify the three-section boundaries and
numbering scheme, but do not constitute a rule-by-rule inventory.

These are candidate search areas derived from the works' chapter organization,
not claims that the sections or individual rules are equivalent. In particular,
Nannūl 204–205 and Tolkāppiyam Puṇariyal sūtras previously found in an
un-pinned online root-text transcription remain provisional leads until checked
against an inspected and explicitly selected Tolkāppiyam edition and its
commentary.

The first milestone is a source and rule inventory, not implementation. The
inventory must cover all three Nannūl Eḻuttatikāram puṇariyal sections. The
first code milestone is merger-only, scoped to a minimal closed subset selected
after inventory and linguistic review; do not preselect a convenient sūtra pair
as representative of the whole grammar. Lexical splitting is deferred until a
separate lexicon review.

A contemporary TN/SL usage profile, including relevant regional conventions,
requires contemporary descriptive or normative evidence and separate fixtures;
neither classical source alone proves modern regional behavior.

## Evidence record required for each rule

Before a rule can drive code or an active golden row, record all of the
following in this specification or its linked data source:

- Stable internal rule ID and source tradition (`tolkappiyam`, `nannool`, or a
  separately named contemporary profile).
- Work, atikāram, iyal, edition/editor/commentator, publisher, year, printed
  page, and the edition's sūtra number. Keep chapter-local numbering explicit.
- A stable source URL and, for a scanned source, the inspected page/image and
  its checksum in the repository's reference-data inventory.
- Evidence layer: root sūtra, commentator gloss, commentator example, modern
  analysis, or project inference. Never silently promote an inference to a
  quoted rule.
- Reviewed statement of the triggering boundary and conditions, output change,
  unchanged cases, exceptions, and whether reverse analysis is ambiguous.
- Whether the rule is descriptive of its historical/literary source or approved
  for a modern usage profile. Historical source coverage alone establishes no
  TN, SL, Jaffna, Batticaloa, Vanni, Up-Country, Kongu, or Chennai convention.
- Named reviewer and review date for linguistic interpretation, plus source
  reuse/redistribution status for any examples or derived lexical entries.

Sūtra wording, commentary, examples, and project-generated examples must remain
distinguishable. Where numbering or readings differ between editions, retain
both citations and do not merge the readings. Golden Tamil strings must be
written as JSON Unicode escapes and inspected by scalar; preserve any joiners
unless an explicit, sourced rule says otherwise.

## Proposed contract boundary (not yet approved for implementation)

- Rule application must name a grammar/source profile. There is no implicit
  universal `punarchi` mode.
- A merger consumes explicitly segmented components and returns the output
  specified by the selected, reviewed rule. It must not guess component
  boundaries from an arbitrary string.
- A future splitter may return zero, one, or multiple analyses. Any lexical
  filtering must use a separately licensed and versioned lexicon. Do not claim
  unique segmentation when multiple analyses are valid.
- Unrecognized cases and invalid profiles fail explicitly. Bindings will call
  the Rust dispatcher; no language binding gets an independent rule engine.
- Historical Nannūl/Tolkāppiyam evidence is not by itself a modern dialect label.
  Region-sensitive claims require separately sourced TN and SL cases.

Names, argument shapes, normalization policy, result ordering, and error text
remain undecided until the rule inventory and lexicon contract are reviewed.

## Existing projects: evidence leads and reuse risks

These projects can help identify edge cases and source references. They are not
the linguistic authority for this module, and their outputs are not golden
expected values. No code, corpus rows, or derived lexicon entries have been
copied. This is a read-only repository review; candidate behavior must still be
re-derived from pinned sources and reviewed fixtures.

| --- | --- | --- |

The repo's [`LICENSE.yaml`](https://github.com/TamilGeekGirl/TamilSandhiNeuroSymbolicAI/blob/main/TamilSandhi/LICENSE.yaml) contains an incomplete MIT heading and non-commercial condition, while [`setup.py`](https://github.com/TamilGeekGirl/TamilSandhiNeuroSymbolicAI/blob/main/TamilSandhi/setup.py) and [PyPI metadata](https://pypi.org/project/tamilsandhi-toolkit/0.1.0/) claim MIT. Each proposed rule also needs an exact primary-text citation and expert review. |
| [tamil-sandhi-checker](https://github.com/nithyadurai87/tamil-sandhi-checker) | Python checker that reports/corrects word-boundary Vallinam usage; source contains inline word lists and a bundled noun list. | README declares GPLv3, so do not port its implementation into this MIT project without a compatible licensing decision or permission. Do not treat it as a validated Punarchi engine or import code/data yet. |
| [thiruppugazh-sandhi-rs](https://github.com/digital-tamil/thiruppugazh-sandhi-rs) | Paired joined/split readings for more than 1,300 Thiruppugazh songs, sourced from Mayuragiri according to its README. | Potential poetry-specific evaluation lead, not general modern-Tamil rules or a lexicon. README declares MIT for code and ODbL for data, but says the split readings come from a separate site; root file listing has no standalone license file. The research paper [A Transformer-Based Sandhi Splitter for Tamil](https://uttamam.org/papers/tic2024.pdf)
is a separate literature lead, not a verified repository or reusable corpus. It
scopes its task to external sandhi in non-compound forms and excludes semantic
compounds from its split targets. This supports keeping lexical segmentation
separate from a deterministic rule merger; it does not provide an approved
lexicon for this project.

Audit rule: upstream tests and model metrics are not substitutes for our shared
golden vectors. Cite each source tradition separately, reproduce disputed cases
only after scalar inspection, and re-check licenses at the exact file/data
level before any reuse.

## Acceptance criteria

### Source audit milestone

- [x] Select Nannūl Eḻuttatikāram as the primary source for this module's
  explicitly Nannūl-scoped merger profile; keep Tolkāppiyam separate.
- [x] Pin a Nannūl root-text/commentary edition and inspect its imprint,
  editorial method, and representative root/commentary pages; keep those
  evidence layers distinct.
- [x] Verify the three Nannūl Eḻuttatikāram puṇariyal section boundaries and
  chapter-local ranges against the Project Madurai contents; confirm Nannūl
  numbering and the Meyyīṟṟup location in the TVA critical edition.
- [x] Inspect the 1979, 2003, and 1982 Tolkāppiyam comparison candidates and
  the 1996 variant study; record their source roles and why no sole comparison
  edition is pinned yet.
- [ ] Obtain the Annamalai/Sundaramurthy 1992 third edition or the CICT
  cataloged 1996 copy; inspect title verso, preface, and relevant readings to
  determine whether it revises or reprints the 1979 witness.
- [ ] Audit the selected Nannūl edition rule by rule, recording the TVA HTML
  page, sūtra number, root reading, commentary, and examples; verify printed
  pagination and variants against a scan or physical copy before fixtures.
  Provisional TVA HTML paraphrases for sūtras 151–157 are in the source notes;
  they do not complete this criterion or approve any rule/example for fixtures.
- [ ] Inventory all rules in the three sections and select a bounded merger
  subset after linguistic review. Compare relevant Tolkāppiyam rules against
  a source selected after candidate-edition review; no rule-level comparison
  is approved yet.
- [x] Review existing implementation leads for scope, reported issues, and
  declared reuse terms; record them as non-authoritative and import nothing.
- [ ] Have a Tamil grammar reviewer approve the rule inventory, interpretation,
  and each source layer. Unreviewed items remain marked unresolved.
- [ ] Keep the first implementation merger-only. Define any later splitter as
  a separate milestone; before that work, approve its lexicon provenance,
  license, redistribution terms, coverage, and versioning.
- [ ] Add sourced, scalar-inspected positive, negative, and ambiguity cases to
  the planned suite only after the preceding evidence exists. Cover the
  selected source profile and explicitly label literary/historical scope;
  add TN/SL regional cases only where separately supported.

### Implementation milestone (separate review)

- [ ] Activate the merger-only `punarchi_nannool.jsonl` suite only after the
  owner reviews the source contract and fixtures. Keep splitter behavior out
  until its separate lexicon milestone passes review. Do not count planned rows
  as passing.
- [ ] Implement only the approved rules in Rust and keep tables/data declarative
  under `lexicon/`; generated code is build output, not hand-maintained data.
- [ ] Run the identical active vectors through Rust, Python, Node/browser WASM,
  and Dart using the shared dispatcher. Unknown operations must fail.
- [ ] Document the supported edition/profile and limits; do not claim complete
  Nannūl, Tolkāppiyam, modern Tamil, or regional coverage from a seed suite.
